# Clinician and Domain-Expert Pilot Invitation Email

**Subject:** Invitation to join the OrthoAI clinical pilot: 20 clinicians and up to 1,000 real-world cases

Dear Clinician and Domain Expert,

We would like to invite you and your clinic to participate in the OrthoAI Research Mode v3 pilot study. The pilot will evaluate how qualified clinicians use AI-supported orthodontic assessment during realistic clinical work. Our goal is not only to measure whether clinicians and AI agree, but to understand when AI is useful, when clinicians appropriately disagree or override it, how confidence changes, and whether the workflow supports safe and efficient decision-making.

## Proposed pilot scale

- Up to **20 licensed clinicians and domain experts** across participating clinics.
- Up to **1,000 eligible real-world patient cases** in total, subject to ethics approval, clinic authorization, patient consent requirements, and data-governance agreements.
- A practical planning target of up to **50 eligible cases per clinician**, although case allocation may vary by clinic and clinical workload.
- Use during genuine clinical assessment tasks so that the HCI study reflects real workflows rather than artificial laboratory behavior.

Please do not upload or enter real patient data until your clinic has received written study-activation confirmation and all applicable institutional, ethics, consent, privacy, and data-processing requirements have been completed.

## What each clinician will do

For each eligible case, the clinician will:

1. Sign in securely using their professional email address and a one-time password.
2. Create a case using a non-identifying patient code and upload properly de-identified clinical images.
3. Allow OrthoAI to process the case.
4. Enter an independent clinical assessment while the AI result remains hidden.
5. Review the OrthoAI result and quantitative evidence.
6. Confirm or update the final clinical assessment and complete a very short follow-up only when requested.
7. Continue to the Cases page, where the diagnosis and research response remain linked and traceable.

The interface intentionally presents the clinician's independent assessment before displaying the AI output. This separation is essential to evaluating AI influence without contaminating the clinician's initial judgment.

## Information collected for the HCI study

Subject to the approved study protocol and participant information sheet, Research Mode may collect:

- A governed participant code, clinic/site code, professional specialty, and experience band.
- Non-identifying case metadata and image modality information.
- The pre-AI assessment, including malocclusion class, IOTN DHC grade, optional IOTN AC grade, intended clinical action, and confidence.
- The exact AI result shown to the clinician, including model version, result provenance, quantitative findings, and timing information.
- The post-AI final assessment, confidence, agreement or disagreement, whether the clinician changed or overrode the AI suggestion, the reason for material changes, and perceived usefulness.
- Research interaction events such as phase start and completion times, active decision time, idle periods, navigation, AI reveal, answer changes, completion, and any voluntary repeat attempt.
- Brief scheduled usability, trust, workload, or AI-influence questions where these are enabled by the approved protocol.
- Technical problems and optional qualitative comments that help improve the clinical workflow.

The system preserves separate pre-AI and post-AI records and does not overwrite completed research observations. A repeated diagnosis or research review is stored as a new, linked attempt so that the original record remains intact.

## Information that must not be entered

Clinicians must not enter or upload patient names, dates of birth, phone numbers, email addresses, full medical-record numbers, unredacted identifying overlays, identifiable facial photographs, or any other direct identifier unless the approved protocol and clinic authorization explicitly permit it. Use only the study-approved patient or case code.

## Clinical safety boundary

OrthoAI is a decision-support research tool and is not a standalone diagnostic system. The participating clinician remains responsible for all clinical decisions. The pilot must not replace local standards of care, mandatory investigations, supervision, documentation, referral pathways, or professional judgment. No treatment decision should be made solely because of an OrthoAI output.

## What we request from participating clinics

We ask each interested clinic to:

- Nominate a local clinical lead or study liaison.
- Identify eligible licensed clinicians or domain experts.
- Confirm the expected number of eligible cases during the pilot period.
- Complete all required local ethics, governance, consent, and data-processing steps before activation.
- Ensure clinical images are de-identified before upload.
- Provide participants with access to a secure workstation and their professional email account.
- Attend a short onboarding session and report safety or technical concerns promptly.
- Avoid sharing accounts; each participant must use their own governed identity.

## Expected clinician commitment

Participation includes a short onboarding session, use of OrthoAI during agreed eligible clinical tasks, completion of the brief Research Mode assessment for each case, and a short end-of-pilot feedback activity. The interface is designed to keep the additional research burden low, and the study will measure actual active time rather than relying only on self-reported duration.
